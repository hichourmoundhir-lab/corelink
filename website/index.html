The problem

Frame-based perception is the ceiling on drone speed.

Cameras blur at speed. GPUs and NPUs add 30–100 ms of queueing, batching, and driver latency. In feedback control, that delay is not an inconvenience — it is the stability margin.

◐

Motion blur

Frame integration smears exactly the detail a reactive controller needs at the moment it matters most.

∿

Latency jitter

GPU/NPU latency scales with batch and queue occupancy — unpredictable, and unfixable in software.

◲

Low dynamic range

Sun, shadow, and glare — the places drones fail most are precisely where frame cameras clip.

The pipeline

A streaming fabric on the camera clock.

Every stage is a hardware pipeline with a composable latency budget. Raw event traffic never touches software — only the low-rate advisory crosses into Linux.

01

Event pre-processing

Noise filtering, hot-pixel removal, timestamp synchronization.

RTL · <50 µs

Time-surface accumulation

HLS · <100 µs
03

Optical flow

Per-pixel occupancy with sweep-consistent worst-case timing under sustained load.

HLS · <200 µs
05

CBF filter + advisory

A control-barrier function encodes a brake/avoid advisory guaranteed from measured worst-case latency.

ARM · <50 µs

How it works

From event to advisory in three lines.

1

Sense

GENX320 event sensor streams asynchronous change events at 320×320 with microsecond timestamps.

2

Process

The FPGA fabric filters, accumulates, and estimates flow entirely in hardware — on the camera clock.

3

Advise

A CBF safety filter emits brake / avoid advisories over MAVLink. The autopilot stays in command.
