Abstract
Commercial and military Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) face severe operational bottlenecks due to open-loop camera capture and high computing latency. Open-loop systems record data blindly, leading to a 20%–30% imagery rejection rate from motion blur and misframing, while GPU-based edge computing introduces non-deterministic OS latency jitter (30ms–100ms) and high power consumption (>15W). This specification presents the Quality Perception Co-Processor: a low-power (5W–7W), heterogeneous FPGA-SoC hardware payload (AMD Kria K26) that executes zero-copy pixel-level quality scoring (Variance of Laplacian) alongside quantized INT8 object detection directly in hardware fabric. Operating under a sub-12ms bounded end-to-end latency, the system generates dynamic MAVLink velocity advisory signals to guarantee >98% usable data yield in commercial utility inspections and robust anti-jamming optical navigation in tactical defense scenarios.
Keywords— FPGA-SoC, Heterogeneous Co-Design, MAVLink, Real-Time Perception, Closed-Loop Speed Control, Variance of Laplacian, Dual-Use UAVs.

1. Introduction & Technical System Context

Modern autonomous UAV operations require tight integration between high-resolution visual sensing and flight control dynamics. In standard commercial and tactical configurations, companion computers act as passive data recorders or execute unconstrained neural network inference on general-purpose GPUs. This architectural decoupled model causes two critical failure modes:

  • Data Rejection Overhead: In high-speed inspection or low-altitude reconnaissance, platform motion and wind gusts induce severe motion blur. Because quality evaluation occurs offline post-flight, operators suffer a 25% re-flight penalty.
  • Latency Non-Determinism: Operating system context switches, DRAM buffer contention, and GPU driver queues introduce random latency jitter (up to 100ms), breaking the real-time constraints required for closed-loop trajectory adjustments.

The proposed Quality Perception Co-Processor resolves these limitations by implementing a streaming hardware pipeline directly between the camera sensor clock and the autopilot serial interface, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

1.1 Mathematical Control Formulation

The FPGA Programmable Logic (PL) computes the spatial Variance of Laplacian ($Q_{ ext{blur}}$) for each incoming frame $I(x,y)$ using a $3 imes3$ discrete convolution operator:

Q_{ ext{blur}}(t) = ext{Var}\left( abla^2 I(t) ight) = rac{1}{N} \sum_{x,y} \left( L(x,y) - ar{L} ight)^2 ag{1}

where $L(x,y) = rac{\partial^2 I}{\partial x^2} + rac{\partial^2 I}{\partial y^2}$. The real-time ARM Cortex-R5 core ingests $Q_{ ext{blur}}(t)$ over AXI registers and computes the advisory velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_{ ext{adv}}(t)$ transmitted via MAVLink to PX4:

\mathbf{v}_{ ext{adv}}(t) = \mathbf{v}_{ ext{nominal}} \cdot \max \left( \gamma_{ ext{min}}, \; 1 - lpha \cdot ext{ReLU}\left( 1 - rac{Q_{ ext{blur}}(t)}{Q_{ ext{target}}} ight) ight) ag{2}

where $lpha$ is the feedback gain constant, $Q_{ ext{target}}$ represents the minimum sharpness threshold, and $\gamma_{ ext{min}} = 0.2$ maintains minimum safe aerodynamic velocity.

3. Academic Literature & State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Review

The proposed architecture synthesizes three distinct bodies of academic research into a unified real-time embedded system:

3.1 Image Quality Evaluation in Hardware

Pech-Pacheco et al. established the Variance of Laplacian ($ ext{VoL}$) as an optimal zero-reference autofocus and blur metric due to its high sensitivity to high-frequency edge degradation. While software implementations on general-purpose CPUs require 15ms–35ms per 4K frame, mapping the $3 imes3$ Laplacian kernel directly into FPGA streaming logic reduces processing time to sub-1ms, operating inline with camera clock rates.

3.2 Perception-Aware Trajectory Control

Falanga et al. (ETH Zurich Robotics & Perception Group) introduced Perception-Aware Model Predictive Control (PAMPC), demonstrating that quadrotor flight trajectories must dynamically account for camera field-of-view and visual tracking quality. However, PAMPC was formulated for high-power desktop GPUs. This project reformulates perception-aware control as a lightweight, low-latency advisory velocity throttling loop executed on a 5W FPGA-SoC.

3.3 Low-Latency FPGA Perception Pipelines

Recent publications in IEEE Transactions on Industrial Electronics demonstrate that FPGA heterogeneous architectures achieve end-to-end perception latencies below 3ms. By combining AXI4-Stream zero-copy memory transfers with quantized INT8 Deep Processing Units (DPU), hardware pipelines eliminate the memory bottleneck inherent in conventional GPU architectures.
